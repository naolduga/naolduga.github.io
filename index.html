Every ledger is a constitution.

My work sits where technology, capital and new forms of money meet the state, the law and markets. That makes it political economy before it is engineering: someone keeps the ledger, someone sets the rules, and someone decides who gets a say.

Every system I build takes a position between centralized, decentralized and distributed. Markets need a center that can be held to account. Resilience needs no single point of failure. The craft is placing authority where it belongs, and making it earned rather than declared. Some of the best designs are old: societies that governed by assembly and rotating leadership long before computers.

Trust

Trust is my longest running interest. I think it should be earned, depend on context, and be checkable, whether it is placed in a person, an organization, or an AI agent acting for one. The work below started with a 2014 paper and runs to what I am building now.

Promise Games2014 paper

My paper P2P Web of Trust Calculi and Promise Games. Its core claims: an agent can only promise its own behavior, and reputation has to be earned. In 2026 I graded it against itself.

TelosResearch

A commitment layer for agents on Ethereum. Each agent is bounded by a stated purpose and issues receipts for the promises it makes. It grows out of the TelosVM idea in the 2014 paper.

It lives at eth.et, my research on ETH as a liminal namespace where Ethiopia’s state trust systems and Ethereum can recognize each other.

TAOResearch

Trustworthy Agentic Organizations, Synestate R&D hosted at the Ethiopian Artificial Intelligence Institute. It ties AI agents to the organizations they act for with verifiable, cryptographic identity.

Interests

  • Market infrastructure

    Exchanges, central securities depositories, custody, clearing and settlement, and the FIX, FAST and ISO 20022 messages that pass between them. The plumbing that makes a market real.

  • Distributed ledgers

    Ethereum, its enterprise clients and tokenized assets, connected to the ISO 20022 rails banks already run. I use DLT where it earns its place: a shared ledger only makes sense when no single party should keep the books.

  • Consensus & governance

    Agreeing without a single ruler is one problem with many solutions, from P2P networks to DLT, and from Kubernetes to DAOs and Gada. Kubernetes means helmsman, from the same root as governor.

  • Agents & agency

    Nietzsche opens the second essay of the Genealogy of Morals with an animal entitled to make promises. Agent trust starts there. Before an AI agent can act for us, it has to be able to promise and be held to its word.

  • Multitude markets

    Plurality organized into structure: Whitman’s “I contain multitudes,” the assembly of multitudes in Asmarom Legesse’s Gada, Medemer (synergy) in Abiy’s political thought, and the prefix syn, as in Synestate.

    Markets are one way a multitude acts together. I want them working for Ethiopia’s five priority sectors: agriculture, manufacturing, mining, tourism and technology.
